# Automatic local-service growth mode

Activate this workflow whenever the project is a location-based service business, even if the prompt only asks to build or redesign the website.

## 1. Verify the business footprint

Collect before keyword research:

- legal/brand name and preferred public name;
- real physical locations versus service-area-only operations;
- verified phone, hours, emergency/after-hours policy, languages, and contact workflow;
- services actually offered, excluded work, minimum jobs, response boundaries, and seasonal constraints;
- licenses, insurance, certifications, warranties, financing, brands, materials, and guarantees only when documented;
- cities, neighborhoods, counties, ZIP/postal areas, and realistic travel radius actually served;
- project photos, case studies, reviews, staff expertise, process, and local proof with publication rights.

Do not turn a service area, mailing address, virtual office, registration ZIP, or Google Business Profile area into a claimed storefront. Do not imply 24/7 response, arrival time, permits, licensure, insurance, or pricing without authorization.

## 2. Research demand and questions

Use live web/search tools. Record source, date, market, and observation. When available, combine:

- search results for `[service]`, `[service] [city]`, `[problem]`, `[service] cost`, `[service] near me`, and urgent modifiers;
- autocomplete, People Also Ask, related searches, forums, review language, trade associations, manufacturer guidance, and local government/permit sources;
- Search Console, Google Business Profile, Bing Webmaster Tools, analytics, call notes, lead forms, and paid-search query data supplied by the owner;
- competitor titles, route coverage, proof, FAQs, gaps, and weak claims without copying their prose;
- keyword tools for volume/difficulty/CPC when accessible; label estimates and never invent metrics.

Classify every query:

| Intent | Examples | Typical destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transactional | emergency locksmith, kitchen remodeling company | Service or conversion page |
| Local | locksmith in Granada, remodeler serving Leon | Qualified location page |
| Commercial investigation | best lock type, remodeling contractor cost | Comparison/service guide |
| Informational/problem | key stuck in lock, permit for bathroom remodel | Helpful article or FAQ section |
| Trust | licensed locksmith, insured remodeling contractor | About, credentials, process, service page |
| Navigational | brand name reviews, brand contact | About/contact/review destination |

Group terms by shared intent, not by superficial wording. A synonym usually belongs on one strong page, not a second competing page.

### Use a repeatable research sample

For each core service and priority market, inspect enough results to identify stable patterns rather than relying on one query. At minimum record:

- the query, locale/device assumptions, date, and visible result types;
- leading local-pack/business categories when visible;
- recurring service terminology, modifiers, questions, and trust factors;
- whether results favor a service page, location page, guide, directory, product, or mixed intent;
- at least three credible competitor or authoritative pages and the useful topics they cover;
- gaps where searchers still lack a clear, locally applicable answer.

Search results are personalized and change. Label conclusions as observations, not universal ranking facts. Do not scrape or automate a source against its terms.

### Score page opportunities

Score each candidate from 0-3 on:

| Dimension | 0 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business fit | Not offered | Core profitable service |
| User demand | No evidence | Repeated evidence across owned/search/tool data |
| Conversion value | Little commercial value | Direct qualified-lead intent |
| Unique evidence | No original material | Strong projects, expertise, photos, or local detail |
| Competitive gap | Results already complete | Clear unmet need or weak local coverage |
| Maintenance ability | Likely to become stale | Owner can keep it accurate |

Prioritize high totals. Reject any candidate with business fit or unique evidence at zero, regardless of keyword volume.

## 3. Create the keyword-to-page map

For each proposed URL record:

```text
URL
Page type
Primary intent
Primary topic phrase
Supporting entities and questions
Target geography
Unique evidence available
Conversion action
Parent/hub
Required inbound links
Useful outbound citations
Schema candidate
Status: create / improve / merge / redirect / reject
```

Give one canonical page ownership of each intent. Merge or redirect overlapping pages. Do not force exact-match wording into every heading, link, or paragraph.

## 4. Build the site architecture

Use only layers the business can support with original content:

```text
Home
Services hub
  Service detail pages
Locations or service areas hub
  Qualified location pages
Projects / case studies
About / credentials / process
Resources / customer questions
Contact / request service
Policies and required legal pages
```

For locksmiths, possible verified service clusters include residential, commercial, lock repair/rekey, lockout, key replacement, access control, safe work, and automotive. Do not add a cluster merely because competitors have it.

For remodelers, possible verified clusters include kitchen, bathroom, additions, whole-home, exterior, accessibility, design-build, and project-planning content. Separate contractor services from unsupported architecture, engineering, permit, or trade claims.

## 5. Gate every location page

Create a location page only when all are true:

1. The business genuinely serves the area.
2. There is meaningful demand or navigation value.
3. The page can include local evidence or genuinely area-specific guidance.
4. The page has a distinct purpose beyond replacing the city name.
5. The site provides a browsable hierarchy and contextual links.

A useful location page may contain:

- service availability and explicit boundaries in that area;
- neighborhoods/communities served when verified;
- realistic logistics, travel, building types, climate/material issues, permitting links, or common local problems;
- completed projects, photos, reviews, response examples, or staff knowledge tied to the area;
- locally relevant questions and answers;
- clear contact/action and links to the relevant services and nearby useful resources.

Reject pages with spun introductions, identical service lists, fake local offices, map embeds as the only local value, keyword-stuffed neighborhood lists, or every service-city Cartesian combination. These are doorway-page risks.

Require at least three forms of unique local utility before publishing, such as a completed project, original local photo, area-specific logistics, verified customer question, building-stock insight, relevant permit/authority link, local testimonial with permission, distinct service boundary, or named neighborhood knowledge. A city name inserted into shared copy does not count.

### Model the business correctly

- **Storefront:** show an address only when customers can visit a real, staffed location during stated hours.
- **Service-area business:** describe verified service areas without presenting the hidden operational address as a customer-facing office.
- **Hybrid:** distinguish visitable hours/location from areas served off-site.
- **Multiple locations:** create a location entity/page only for a real eligible operation with its own accurate details and evidence, not for a technician, virtual office, or ranking target.

Keep website facts, Organization/LocalBusiness schema, contact pages, and Google Business Profile information consistent. Google-supported `LocalBusiness` rich-result fields expect a physical business location; do not fabricate an address to satisfy schema. Use Organization/Service/entity markup that accurately describes a service-area business when a LocalBusiness representation would mislead.

## 6. Answer the public's real questions

Create an answer bank organized by decision stage:

- **Immediate problem:** What should I do now? What should I avoid? Is it safe?
- **Qualification:** Do you handle this job/property/lock/material? What information do you need?
- **Cost:** What affects price? What is included? What can change the estimate?
- **Timing:** How long can it take? What affects scheduling or completion?
- **Process:** What happens before, during, and after service?
- **Trust:** Who performs the work? What credentials or proof can be verified?
- **Alternatives:** Repair or replace? Rekey or new lock? Remodel or refinish?
- **Local:** Are permits, access, property types, weather, parking, or building rules relevant?
- **Aftercare:** Maintenance, warranty, documentation, and next steps.

Answer directly in the first sentence, then explain conditions and exceptions. Cite authoritative sources for safety, code, permit, manufacturer, or legal/regulatory statements. Do not provide dangerous bypass instructions for locks/security systems or personalized legal/code conclusions.

FAQ content can be valuable even when FAQ rich results are unavailable. Never add `FAQPage` schema merely to chase a rich result; use schema only when it accurately represents visible content and current eligibility.

## 7. Build contextual internal links

Every important page needs at least one crawlable `<a href>` link from another relevant page. Design links by user need:

- Home -> priority services and markets
- Service hub -> every service page
- Service page -> related locations, case studies, process, cost/decision guides, and contact
- Location hub -> every qualified location page
- Location page -> services available there, local proof, neighboring relevant areas, and contact
- Article/question -> the service that solves the problem and one related guide
- Case study -> service, location, and relevant technique/material page
- Footer -> stable high-value navigation, not a keyword dump

Use concise descriptive anchor text in natural sentences. Avoid repeated exact-match anchors, "click here," giant city link grids, and orphan pages. Produce an internal-link matrix and crawl the built site to verify links and status codes.

## 8. Make the site discoverable by search and AI systems

Build common foundations first:

- meaningful server-rendered/static HTML;
- stable canonical URLs and correct status codes;
- unique titles, headings, descriptions, and answer-first passages;
- Organization/LocalBusiness, Service, BreadcrumbList, Article, ImageObject, and other schema only where facts and page content support them;
- clear About, service, location, author/reviewer, contact, and policy information;
- descriptive internal links, XML sitemap with accurate `lastmod`, RSS/feed when appropriate, and useful external citations;
- original photos/media with context, captions where useful, and descriptive alt text;
- visible update/review dates when meaningful and honest;
- concise tables, lists, definitions, and Q&A blocks that remain understandable when quoted out of context.

For ChatGPT search discovery, confirm `OAI-SearchBot` is not blocked by robots.txt, Cloudflare WAF, bot rules, authentication, geo restrictions, or JavaScript challenges on public pages. Decide `GPTBot` training access separately with the owner; search discovery and training controls are different.

For Bing/Copilot discovery, maintain crawlable links and canonical-only sitemaps and consider IndexNow for added, updated, or deleted URLs. Submission improves discovery speed but never guarantees crawling, indexing, ranking, or citation.

Treat `llms.txt` as optional experimental metadata, not a ranking or citation guarantee. Do not substitute it for crawlable pages, schema, sitemaps, internal links, or crawler access.

Google does not use `llms.txt` for Search or its generative features. Do not create it as a default Google SEO task. If another named system documents support and the owner wants it, generate it from canonical public content and keep it synchronized.

## 9. Integrate design and motion

Do not let SEO architecture produce a templated wall of text. Give service and location page families distinct but consistent compositions using relevant photography, process diagrams, project proof, local details, answer accordions, comparisons, and focused conversion sections.

Use professional motion to clarify hierarchy and process: hero choreography, service transitions, before/after comparisons, project galleries, step progress, map/service-area reveals, and tactile calls to action. Do not animate emergency information, phone access, prices, or critical answers in ways that delay use.

## 10. Required deliverables

Before implementation or major rewrite, produce:

- business fact and claim ledger;
- service/location inventory;
- dated keyword and SERP research notes;
- clustered question bank;
- keyword-to-page map;
- site tree and URL plan;
- location-page qualification table;
- internal-link matrix;
- metadata/schema plan;
- AI crawler and indexing policy;
- content briefs with proof/source requirements;
- measurement plan for rankings, impressions, clicks, calls/forms, indexed pages, AI referrals, and lead quality.

The keyword-to-page map must include the opportunity score and evidence source. The location qualification table must include the three unique local-utility items or a `reject/defer` decision.
